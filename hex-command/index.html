Rules — hex adaptation

This is an original 9×9 hex-board adaptation of classic hidden-rank capture-the-flag strategy. Each side has 27 pieces arranged across three home rows, and two blocked lake hexes interrupt the central terrain. The goal is to capture the enemy Flag or leave the opponent with no legal move.

During setup, tap two friendly pieces to swap their places or shuffle your formation. Your opponent cannot see your ranks. In two-player mode, a privacy screen appears between turns.

Each turn, move one piece to an adjacent hex in any of the six directions. Scouts (2) may slide along any of those six straight directions until blocked by another unit or lake, including attacking the first enemy reached. Flags and Bombs cannot move.

In battle, higher rank wins; equal ranks both disappear. Miner (3) defuses a Bomb; other attackers are defeated by it. Spy (1) defeats a Marshal (10) only when the Spy attacks. Attacking a Flag wins immediately. A piece may not repeatedly reverse between the same two hexes beyond the repetition limit.

These are the classic tactical interactions translated to hex geometry, with a reduced army and two lake cells. Computer play uses basic tactical heuristics rather than a perfect solver.
